Equivariant Graph Neural Networks for Room-Temperature Phosphorescence Property Prediction

Bachelor Thesis
Supervisor: Prof. Jun Jiang Fall 2025 – Spring 2026

Problem

Predict key excited-state properties of organic RTP molecules — ΔE(S₁−T₁), spin-orbit coupling, and oscillator strength — from molecular structure alone, using 3D geometric information rather than 1D sequence representations.

Method

  • 8-layer EGNN_Sparse_Network with SE(3)-equivariant message passing
  • 21-dim atom node features, 6-dim bond edge features, explicit 3D coordinates
  • Character-level SMILES Transformer baseline for comparison
  • Rotation/translation augmentation during training

Results (Test-set R², ~6,000 molecules)

0.8781ΔE(S₁−T₁)
0.6263SOC(T₁→S₁)
0.9855SOC(T₁→S₀)
0.7844fosc,S₁

MultiSpec-GeoDiff: Geometry-Constrained Multimodal Spectra-to-Structure Retrieval

Research Demo
2025 – 2026 Prototype / Research Proposal

Problem

Infer plausible molecular structural candidates from multimodal spectroscopic evidence (IR, Raman, NMR, MS). This is an inverse problem: given spectra, recover the geometry-consistent molecular structure.

Method

  • Coordinate-aware spectral encoder with intra-modal attention and spectral coordinate bias
  • Cross-modal fusion across multiple spectroscopic modalities
  • Candidate retrieval and reranking pipeline with forward physical validation
  • Path toward geometric generative extension (GeoDiff-style) for structure proposal

Doublet-Emissive Molecular Design and Synthesis

Undergraduate Research
Supervisor: Prof. Linsong Cui Fall 2023 – Fall 2024

Problem

Design and synthesize novel doublet-emissive organic light-emitting materials. TTM-based radicals show strong doublet emission but limited tunability — can acceptor functionalization redshift emission while preserving quantum yield?

Method

  • Designed TTM-AnQ radical: TTM donor coupled with 9,10-anthraquinone acceptor
  • Multi-step synthesis: Friedel–Crafts, Suzuki coupling, oxidation
  • Characterization: UV-Vis, fluorescence, CV, transient fluorescence
  • First systematic study of acceptor-functionalized radical emitters

Key Findings

580 → 695 nmEmission Redshift
−2.82 → −3.18 eVSOMO Modulation

Multiscale Molecular Simulation for Physical Chemistry Teaching Innovation

Competition · First Prize
Fall 2024 – Spring 2025 Central China Division, Digital Intelligence Track

Problem

Bridge the gap between theoretical physical chemistry and experimental laboratory practice. Use MD and DFT simulations to reveal the microscopic origin of the infinite dilution extrapolation method for dipole moment measurement.

Method

  • MD simulations (GROMACS) for n-butanol/cyclohexane solution-phase behavior
  • DFT calculations (Gaussian 16, B3LYP/6-31G+g(d,p)) for electronic properties
  • 100 trajectory frames extracted for DFT dipole moment computation
  • Experimental validation against measured dipole moments

Results

1.78 DPredicted Dipole
1.66 DExperimental
6.0%Deviation
