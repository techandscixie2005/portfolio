Molecular machine learning / Selected work 02

EGNN for RTP Property Prediction

Geometry-aware learning of excited-state molecular properties, evaluated against a sequence baseline.

Role
Undergraduate Thesis · Advisor: Prof. Jun Jiang
Period
Fall 2025 – Spring 2026
Project type
Research case study

Question & contribution

I studied whether molecular geometry can improve the prediction of excited-state properties relevant to organic room-temperature phosphorescence (RTP). I built the 3D graph pipeline, implemented an eight-layer EGNN, and compared it with a character-level SMILES Transformer under the same split. This was my undergraduate thesis, supervised by Prof. Jun Jiang.

Reported results

The dataset contains approximately 6,000 molecules in total: 4,800 training, 1,050 validation, and 150 test molecules. The values below are the reported EGNN test-set results for those 150 molecules.

Model & comparison

3D geometry → molecular properties

Conceptual schematic
  • Eight-layer EGNN_Sparse_Network with 21-dimensional atom features, six-dimensional bond features, and three-dimensional coordinates.
  • Equivariant message passing encodes molecular geometry; invariant aggregation produces scalar property predictions.
  • A character-level SMILES Transformer provides a sequence baseline on the same training, validation, and test molecules.
  • Rotation and translation augmentation was applied only during EGNN training.

The lowest reported EGNN R² is for SOC(T₁→S₁), at 0.6263.

Scope & Limitations

These isolated-molecule targets do not directly predict solid-state device performance, experimental quantum yield, or phosphorescence lifetime; crystal packing and intermolecular effects are outside this evaluation. Public baseline scores, raw predictions, and uncertainty estimates are unavailable, so the reported EGNN results do not establish the magnitude or consistency of improvement over the baseline.
