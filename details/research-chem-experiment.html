Computational chemistry / Supporting work

Multiscale Molecular Simulation

Linking molecular dynamics, DFT, and a physical chemistry experiment to explain dipole measurements.

Role
Undergraduate Competition Project
Period
Fall 2024 – Spring 2025
Project type
Teaching innovation / competition

Question & contribution

How can molecular simulation help explain a physical chemistry experiment? For an undergraduate competition project, I built an experiment-and-computation workflow linking n-butanol/cyclohexane mixtures, molecular dynamics, and electronic-structure calculations.

From solution to molecular properties

  1. Construct two solution models. Compare 17:400 and 1:400 n-butanol/cyclohexane systems.
  2. Sample the molecular environment. Use GROMACS for energy minimization, equilibration, and production simulation at 30 °C.
  3. Calculate electronic properties. Extract 100 trajectory frames from the dilute system and use Gaussian 16 for DFT dipole calculations.
  4. Interpret the experiment. Examine the molecular basis of infinite-dilution extrapolation and the influence of intermolecular interactions.

Results & interpretation

The project report gives a 1.78 D mean calculated dipole moment from the sampled dilute-system structures. It separately cites a 1.66 D reference value for n-butanol. These values have different comparison contexts in the available records, so no relative-error percentage is claimed here.

This was a teaching-oriented study of one solvent system. It illustrates a route from molecular environments to measured quantities; it does not establish broad predictive accuracy across solvents.

Recognition & evidence

First Prize, Central China Division, 5th National Undergraduate Chemistry Experiment Innovation Design Competition, Digital Intelligence Track (2025).

The methods and original values are documented in the project report and summarized in the CV. No public source repository or independent reproduction is linked for this project.
