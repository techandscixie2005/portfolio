Photophysics / Supporting work

Doublet-Emissive Molecular Design

Design, synthesis, and characterization of an acceptor-functionalized radical emitter.

Role
Undergraduate Research · Supervisor: Prof. Linsong Cui
Period
Fall 2023 – Fall 2024
Project type
Research case study

Question & contribution

I investigated acceptor functionalization as a way to tune radical emission. Under Prof. Linsong Cui’s supervision, I designed and synthesized TTM-AnQ by coupling a TTM radical framework with a 9,10-anthraquinone acceptor, and characterized its optical and electrochemical behavior.

Synthesis & characterization

The multistep route used Friedel–Crafts chemistry, Suzuki coupling, and oxidation. Characterization included UV–Vis absorption, fluorescence, cyclic voltammetry, and transient fluorescence measurements.

Reported observations

Emission peak580 → 695 nm

Redshift after acceptor incorporation.

Estimated SOMO energy−2.82 → −3.18 eV

Energy estimates derived in the project report.

The observations support a possible emission-tuning route through acceptor functionalization and SOMO stabilization. The project also observed limitations in electrochemical stability.

Scope & evidence

This was an exploratory undergraduate study of one molecular system, completed through the Undergraduate Research Program. It does not establish device performance or general behavior across radical emitters. The measurements and energy estimates are recorded in the completion report; no public raw spectra or code release is linked here.
